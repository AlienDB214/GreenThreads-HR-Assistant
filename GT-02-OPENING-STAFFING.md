# GT-02 - Denver Opening, Staffing Model, and Client Contacts

## Denver Store Operating Model

The Denver location is a 3,200 sq ft store operating seven days a week, roughly 11 hours a day. That is about 77 store-hours to cover, with two to four people on the floor at any time. It takes 14 people:

| Role | Headcount | Type | Reports to |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Store Manager | 1 | Full-time | Regional lead (HQ) |
| Assistant Manager | 2 | Full-time | Store Manager |
| Sales Associate | 8 | Mostly part-time | Assistant Manager |
| Stock Associate | 2 | Part-time | Assistant Manager |
| Visual Merchandiser | 1 | Part-time | Store Manager |
| **Total** | **14** | | |

Sales Associate is the whole problem: 8 of the 14 seats, the highest turnover, and the hardest to fill fast. Seven of the 14 are already accepted. Seven are not, and the clock is at 90 days.

## Client Contacts

**Marcus - CEO.** Gut-driven, expansion-focused. He set the clock. He's the audience for the final presentation, and he wants to hear that Denver opens.

**Jennifer - CFO, second-in-command.** Data-driven, cautious. She controls the money, signs off on the team's work, and asks how the team knows. GreenThreads raised a Series B eighteen months ago, so she answers to a board that expects discipline instead of vibes.

They are not aligned. That's the engagement. The team's PM is the single point of contact to Jennifer. All questions, updates, and sign-off run through the PM.

## Structural Constraint

The Marcus/Jennifer tension is structural. Do not resolve it - work with it.
